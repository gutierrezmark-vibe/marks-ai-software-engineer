Scale & frets
Scale type
Perpendicular fret
Scale lengths are clamped to 23–31 in (584.2–787.4 mm). For multiscale, the perpendicular fret is held square to the centerline. Nut: frets fan out toward the bridge. A middle fret: that fret is square and both ends fan. Bridge: the fan reverses, widest at the nut. Treble = high-E edge (shorter); bass = low-string edge (longer).
Step geometry
At every fret position the board has a square edge that stands up by the step height — that edge does the job of a fret, and it's what the string presses against. Ramp up is the original Bond Electraglide: from the nut, the surface starts low and climbs to the top of fret 1, drops straight down, then climbs again to fret 2, and so on. The last fret ends at full height and drops to the heel. Ramp down is the reverse: each edge stands up on the bridge side and the surface ramps down toward the next fret, with a flat run from the nut to fret 1. Full height is the board at its thickest (the top of any step), measured from the flat back. The thinnest point is the bottom of each ramp: full height minus step height. Most boards land around 5–6 mm full height.
Board taper STEP · STL
Number of strings sets typical nut and heel widths for that many strings (6: 43/57, 7: 48/66, 8: 56/74, 9: 63/82 mm); adjust either width afterwards to match your string spacing. Width runs from the nut width at fret 0 to the heel width at the end of the board, centered on the length axis. For multiscale, the treble edge is the back face and the bass edge is the front face; the solid lofts between them. Set nut = heel with single scale for a plain parallel extrusion.
Fingerboard radius STEP · STL
The radius is a cylinder whose axis runs along the neck, so the curve is across the width; the side profile is unchanged. The crown keeps the full base thickness and the edges thin out by the sagitta. Minimum 7.25 in (184.15 mm). The STEP solid uses true curved surfaces (one smooth face per ramp, like a curved loft). Arc facets only set how smooth the STL and the 3D preview are. Flat mode STEP is mathematically exact.
